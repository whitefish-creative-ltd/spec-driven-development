> software, you don't have a problem yet, you have a preference.

## 1. The problem

What is happening that shouldn't be, or not happening that should? Describe it without
mentioning any solution, technology, or feature.

## 2. Who has it

Who specifically. Not "users". Which people, in which role, doing what, how often.

## 3. Evidence

How do we know this is real? Numbers, tickets, recordings, complaints, revenue. If the only
evidence is that someone senior said so, write that down, it's still evidence, just weaker
than it feels in the room.

## 4. Cost of the problem

What does it cost today, in money, time, risk, or churn? An estimate with a stated basis beats
a confident number with none.

## 5. Why now

What changed? If nothing changed, this has been tolerable for a while and probably still is.

## 6. What happens if we do nothing

Be honest. Sometimes the answer is "very little", and that's a result worth having early.

## 7. Constraints known up front

Budget, deadline, regulatory, technical, political. Anything that narrows the solution space
before we start exploring it.

## 8. What success looks like

How would we know the problem is gone? Stated as an observable change in the world, not a
feature shipped.

## 9. Open questions

Things we don't know that would change the answer. Owner and date for each.
